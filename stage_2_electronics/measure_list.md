## Safety first

**Before any entry: Q1 off does not make the cabinet dead.** With Q1 at 0, XP, the mains filter ZF1, the breaker FA4 and the socket XC7 stay live (OM p.12, m004236; spec/power.md section 2). The NOARK pair may stay live too, since its supply is unknown (entry P24 below). Isolate the machine upstream (the wall isolator, or the distribution-board breaker or fuses that feed XP), lock it off, and test for dead at XP before any work in the cabinet. The inverter UZ1 keeps its DC bus (+UG/-UG, entry P35) charged for a while after isolation: wait for the time its label or manual gives, then measure before touching. Among the power entries (P), P29 is the only measurement on live parts. It is for a qualified electrician with a meter and test leads rated for mains work (measurement category III or higher). (Copied from spec/power.md section 9, ids given their letter; spec/control.md section 11 says the same.)

**Work with the supply on: 9 entries, P29, C5, C10, C12, C13, C14, C17, C18, C21.** P29 is the measurement on live mains parts above. C13, C17 and C21 start the machine (M1, a cut from the two-hand buttons) while the cabinet is open and a meter fixed beforehand reads: the knife and the clamp can move, so nobody reaches into the cabinet, the knife opening or the clamp zone while the machine is on. C14 and C18 run with control voltage only, as each row defines it. C5, C10 and C12 are read with the supply on, from a lamp, an LED or a meter fixed while the machine was isolated. Each of these rows is marked LIVE and gives its own method: fix every meter lead while the machine is isolated, keep hands clear while the supply is on, and isolate again before touching anything. (Built by the script from the rows marked LIVE; the other rows are made with the machine isolated.)

## How to read this list

- x, y, z: millimetres in the machine frame of the 3D model, the same frame as stage 1 (stage_1_mechanics). X runs along the knife, + to the operator's right (the operator stands at the front table, facing the machine); Y + from the cutting line toward the backgauge (rear); Z up. X 0 = middle of the cutting width, Y 0 = cutting line (where the knife edge meets the cutting stick), Z 0 = floor (underside of the feet), so every z in this list, "where" included, is a height above the floor. X 0 is itself an estimate (±20 mm, stage-1 measure row frame.M2): mark the middle of the cutting width first and measure each x from that mark. The model stands at rest: knife up, clamp up, backgauge pusher face at Y 1150, so a device on the knife, the clamp or the backgauge carriage has its x, y, z only in that pose. (Sources: stage_1_mechanics/spec/frame.md lines 15-17; stage_1_mechanics/spec/knife.md line 4; stage_1_mechanics/spec/clamp.md line 4; stage_1_mechanics/spec/backgauge.md line 3.)
- One table per place: cabinet, console, machine, and a last table for entries between places or in the documents. An entry whose devices, boxes or cable ends all have an area (below) goes to the table of their place, or to the last table when they lie in two places. Otherwise the place comes from the place column of the netlist device rows the entry names (for P, C and D entries the devices or the place word their text names), and an entry naming devices of two places goes to the last table.
- area (walk order): where the entry is, taken from the 3D scheme (scheme_3d/placements.csv) for the device, box or cable end the entry names. Each table runs in one walk, so a technician passes each area once: cabinet: door, switch plate, plate rows 1 (top) to 7 (bottom), left wall, floor, the wall toward the machine (cable entries), then its outside face (connectors XC1-XC4); console: left, middle, right part as the operator sees it, each part inside then outside; machine: the front (operator side) from left to right, the right side from front to rear, the rear (backgauge end) from right to left, the left side from rear to front, last the middle of the knife and clamp zone (under the covers). In the cabinet the area is the plate row as photographed (photo_row column), left to right as seen through the door; "by height" means no photo row is given, so the nearest plate row by height Z is named. On the console and the machine the area comes from X and Y of the device: left and right of X ±280 mm on the console and X ±350 mm on the machine, the front below Y -150 mm, the rear above Y 600 mm. An entry with two ends (a wire, a cable leg) shows its areas in walk order (A -> B) and sorts by the end that comes first in the walk, after the entries of that area alone. A device without a box of its own (a plug, a suppressor, a part on a board) is sought at the device its netlist row says it sits across, on board or on ("across Y3", "on board UG50", "on the PLC"), else at the device of the same place it has most conductors to, and takes that device's area. For P, C and D entries the area comes from every device their key and place text name (a name right after another device's name, such as E3 in "UZ1 E3", is a terminal of that device and is skipped); "rail 4" there is the plate rail mount.rail.R4, which carries plate row 4. "no 3D place" means the entry names no device the 3D scheme places; such entries come last in their table.
- where: for L, L+ (placed boxes), N, F and X entries each device the entry names, on what it is fastened to and at which height z in mm (placements.csv), or "no box in the 3D scheme" with the device it is sought at; for a cable leg its two ends; for P, C and D entries the place the spec gives, then ("in the 3D scheme:") the devices its text names, each on its carrier and height, at most four. A carrier written with dots is the body of the 3D model the device is fastened to, part first: mount.plate is the cabinet's mounting plate, mount.rail.R4 its rail 4 (plate row 4), housing.cabinet the cabinet itself, and a machine name such as safety_sensors.S10 or clamp.brake_y14 the machine part at that device (the area column says where it is). Notes quote the netlists of the drawings: power (568 sheets 1-3), control (sheets 4-7), PLC and drive (sheet 8).
- current value, reason: for L and L+ first what the 3D scheme holds now ("now in the 3D scheme": a box with x, y, z in mm, its size, what it is fastened to and its photo row; a cable leg with its length, diameter, clips and what they hold on to, and the openings it passes), then the stage-1 measure rows that move it (L) or the evidence and note of the row (L+); for N and F the evidence and the netlist note, while the key column holds the wire and its terminals as the netlist has them now; for X the values the netlists give; for P, C and D the reason or the method the spec gives.
- id: P = spec/power.md section 9, C = spec/control.md section 11, D = spec/plc_drive.md section 11, L = spec/layout_3d.md section 11 (placements and bundles of the 3D scheme), L+ = a placements or bundles row not plain sure that the layout list leaves out, N = a netlist row not plain sure (then the netlist: p = power, c = control, d = PLC and drive; then the file: d = devices or w = wires; then the line in that file, so Ndw9 is line 9 of netlist/plc_drive_wires.csv), F = a netlist row whose note compares the 568 print with a photo (same letters), X = a device field two netlists give different values for (merged_conflicts.csv line).
- references inside a cell: "entry P11" or "same as P11" is the entry with that id in this list; a wire key "from -> to (power_wires.csv line 99)" gives the wire's line in that file of the netlist folder, stage_2_electronics/netlist (header = line 1), and a following "(line 100)" is a line of the same file.
- likely error, one of these: the ± the source gives, in mm unless a unit is written (a column is a column of the 568 sheet), often after "estimated" and with the source's words on what it covers; "[?] (unclear)" or "[?] ..." where the source marks the row unclear; "[?]: photo and print differ" where the cabinet photo and the 1998 print disagree and the check decides which holds; "[?]: two netlists give different values" for X entries, where the check decides between the values in the current value column; "reading or yes/no check (no ±)" for a wiring, nameplate or presence check, where a ± has no meaning; "no ± in the spec: measure it" where a place, length or height is asked for and the spec gives no error. No ± is made up where the source gives none.
- moves a device or route: yes for every placements or bundles row (the 3D scheme changes), may where the entry asks for a place, position, route, length or height, no for wiring and nameplate checks.
